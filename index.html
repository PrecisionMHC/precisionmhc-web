The problem

A promising target.
Can a therapy find it?

Cells present peptides from intracellular protein fragments held in MHCs on their surface. Immunotherapies recognize tumor cells by specific peptide targets presented on pMHC complexes. Understanding what targets are present, their abundance, and their tumor specificity is critical in therapeutic development and matching patients to the right treatment.

MATCHING TARGET

T cell recognizes the target and kills the tumor cell.

NO MATCHING TARGET

T cell does not find its target and passes by.

Our approach

From cells and tissues
to clearer targets.

We use mass spectrometry and synthetic peptide standards to identify the peptides presented by MHC and quantify selected targets in tissue or cell line samples, with 5-20x lower input. Whether you’re exploring new targets, evaluating a candidate, or comparing how presentation changes across samples, we turn those measurements into results your team can use.

Why it matters

Better evidence for developers.
Better possibilities for patients.

Finding a target is only the beginning. Validating the target is real, measuring how much is displayed on tumor cells, and whether it also appears in healthy samples helps teams assess target suitability and selectivity, and informs safety studies. Answering these questions earlier supports clearer development decisions and better matching between therapies and patients.
